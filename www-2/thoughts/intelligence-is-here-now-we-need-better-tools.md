I've been building on language models since the davinci days in 2021, back when getting a coherent paragraph felt like a magic trick. So believe me when I say: the intelligence problem, for the vast majority of real-world work, is solved.

Sit today's frontier models down with a well-specified task and they perform at the level of a strong professional. That was the hard part. It took humanity roughly seventy years of computing to get here.

And yet, agents in production still disappoint. Why?

## The bottleneck moved and nobody told the roadmap

When a brilliant new hire underperforms, you don't conclude they lack intelligence. You look at their environment: Do they have access to the systems they need? Can they find the context? Do they know what "done" looks like? Is anyone giving them feedback?

Agents today are brilliant new hires onboarded into the worst workplace imaginable. No credentials — or worse, someone pasted the company's root password into their prompt. No filesystem of institutional knowledge — just whatever fits in the context window. No way to try something, fail safely, and retain the lesson. Every morning, total amnesia.

We keep buying a smarter hire when we should be fixing the office.

## What "better tools" actually means

The gap isn't one missing product; it's a missing *layer*. My shortlist, which is also more or less my project list:

- **Capabilities that persist.** When an agent figures out how to do something, that solution should become callable software — instant and deterministic on every subsequent call — not a reasoning trace to be re-derived at every run.
- **Safe credential handling.** Agents acting on your behalf without ever holding your secrets.
- **Real infrastructure access.** Deploying, registering domains, accepting payments — programmatically, without a human clicking through dashboards.
- **Runtime learning loops.** A structured way to try, fail, iterate, and *keep* the result.

None of this requires a research breakthrough. It's plumbing. Unglamorous, load-bearing plumbing.

## The 1995 moment

We're in the equivalent of 1995 for the web: the core technology works, and everything around it is missing. Nobody in 1995 said "HTTP is not intelligent enough." They built Apache, MySQL, Stripe, AWS.

The equivalent companies for the agent era are being founded right now. The founders who win won't be the ones waiting for GPT-6 to fix their product. They'll be the ones who took today's intelligence seriously and built it a place to work.

Intelligence is here. It's waiting on us now.
